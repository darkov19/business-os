# Business OS Scope

Status: Draft for discussion
Purpose: Define the scope, boundaries, design principles, and coverage expectations for the second version of the Business OS.

## 1. Why This Exists

Version 1 established the right foundation:

- modular operating system
- intent-routed workflows
- decision-area modules
- artifact-driven outputs
- explicit decision gates

The first stress test confirmed that this works well for:

- Chitr by Aalekh
- physical product brands
- pivot scenarios
- launch and expansion logic for product-led businesses

Version 2 expands the scope so the OS is more broadly useful across:

- local service businesses
- SaaS and AI product businesses
- education and coaching businesses
- import and channel-heavy businesses
- businesses with strong retention, support, and capital-planning needs

---

## 2. Core Goal

Build a reusable Business OS that can:

- adapt to the user's intent and business context
- ask enough questions before giving strategic advice
- distinguish facts from assumptions and evidence
- produce practical business documents
- apply decision gates before major recommendations
- support future AI skills and workflows

---

## 3. Primary Use Cases

The OS should support users who want to:

1. start a business from scratch
2. validate an early idea
3. pivot an existing business
4. launch a new product or service
5. improve sales or marketing
6. fix pricing, unit economics, or financial pressure
7. improve operations or delivery
8. evaluate a new category, channel, geography, or adjacency
9. document a business properly
10. diagnose why a business is stuck
11. improve retention, support, or post-sale performance
12. assess whether the business can support new growth financially

---

## 4. Design Principles

### 4.1 Adaptive, Not Static

The system should adapt to the user's query, not force a universal sequence.

### 4.2 Ask Before Telling

Major outputs should be preceded by targeted discovery.

### 4.3 Evidence Over Assumption

The OS should clearly label:

- known facts
- founder assumptions
- weak signals
- validated evidence

### 4.4 Modular By Decision Area

The OS should remain structured around business decisions, not business-plan chapters.

### 4.5 Guided By Entry Workflow

Users should still be able to enter through natural intents such as start, pivot, launch, improve, or expand.

### 4.6 Reusable Across Business Types

The universal core should stay lean, with overlays handling business-specific complexity.

### 4.7 AI-Agent Compatible

Every module should later support:

- discovery questions
- research logic
- evidence requirements
- outputs
- decision gates

### 4.8 Document-Centered

The system should leave behind durable artifacts, not only conversational advice.

### 4.9 Financially Grounded

The OS should not stop at pricing. It must include working capital, cash strain, and capital allocation logic.

### 4.10 Full-Lifecycle Oriented

The OS should cover:

- pre-sale
- sale
- post-sale
- retention
- expansion

---

## 5. Non-Goals

The Business OS should not:

- act like a generic motivational startup guide
- generate polished business plans too early
- assume every business scales through the same channels
- replace specialist legal, tax, or regulatory advice
- treat weak evidence as strategy
- recommend growth that the business cannot operationally or financially support

---

## 6. System Architecture

The Business OS keeps the same six-layer architecture:

1. Intent Layer
2. Context Layer
3. Discovery Layer
4. Decision Module Layer
5. Artifact Layer
6. Agent Execution Layer

Version 2 expands the coverage inside those layers rather than changing the architecture itself.

---

## 7. Must-Cover Decision Areas

The OS should now cover all of the following:

1. Founder Context And Intent
2. Current State And Pivot Diagnosis
3. Business Definition
4. Problem / Opportunity Definition
5. Customer Research
6. Market, Competitor, And Alternatives Research
7. Positioning And Value Proposition
8. Offer Architecture
9. Costing, Pricing, And Unit Economics
10. Operations, Delivery, And Supply System
11. Brand, Messaging, And Trust Assets
12. Route-To-Market And Sales Architecture
13. Marketing And Growth System
14. Launch And Execution Planning
15. Legal, Finance, Risk, And Setup
16. Metrics, Review Loops, And Pivot Rules
17. Financial Planning And Capital Allocation
18. Customer Success And Retention System

---

## 8. Must-Cover Overlay Areas

Version 2 should explicitly support overlays for:

1. Asset And Capability Audit
2. Productization / Service Standardization
3. Inventory, Fulfillment, And Packaging
4. B2B Pipeline And Account Strategy
5. Distributor / Wholesale Readiness
6. Import / Sourcing Evaluation
7. Expansion / New Category Smoke Test
8. Offline Retail / Exhibition Readiness
9. Founder Dependency And Delegation Risk
10. SaaS / Digital Product
11. Local Service Operations
12. Education / Coaching
13. Marketplace / Platform Strategy
14. Experimentation And Pilot Design
15. Organization And Team Design

---

## 9. Business-Type Coverage Expectations

Version 2 should be reliably useful for:

- physical product businesses
- service businesses
- studio / creative practices
- ecommerce-led brands
- B2B / project-led businesses
- distributor / channel-led businesses
- hybrid businesses
- local service businesses
- SaaS / AI product businesses
- education / coaching businesses

It does not yet aim to fully specialize in:

- healthcare
- regulated finance
- franchise systems
- deep industrial manufacturing
- complex multi-country compliance-heavy models

---

## 10. Required Output Quality

The OS should produce outputs that are:

- practical
- modular
- evidence-aware
- easy for future agents to reuse
- explicit about uncertainty
- useful for real business execution
