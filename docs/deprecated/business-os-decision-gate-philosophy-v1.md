# Business OS Decision Gate Philosophy v1

Status: Draft for discussion
Purpose: Define how the Business OS should decide when a business idea, decision, workflow, or artifact is ready to move forward, when it should proceed with warnings, and when it should stop and ask for validation first.

---

## 1. Why Decision Gates Matter

The Business OS should not behave like a content machine.

It should behave like a disciplined operating system.

That means it must do more than:

- ask questions
- run research
- generate documents

It must also decide whether the quality of thinking is strong enough to support the next step.

Without decision gates, the system will produce:

- polished but fragile strategies
- business plans built on weak assumptions
- launch recommendations without readiness
- expansion advice without economic proof

Decision gates are what make the OS trustworthy.

---

## 2. Core Principle

The system should help the user move forward, but it should not pretend uncertainty does not exist.

So the OS should operate with explicit gate outcomes rather than hidden judgment.

---

## 3. Gate Outcomes

Every major module or workflow should be able to end in one of five states.

### G1. Proceed

Meaning:

- the decision is sufficiently supported
- key prerequisites are present
- no major blockers are visible

Use when:

- evidence is reasonably strong
- operational logic is coherent
- major risks are understood

### G2. Proceed With Warnings

Meaning:

- progress is acceptable, but important uncertainties remain

Use when:

- the user needs momentum
- some assumptions remain open
- the next step is still worthwhile if warnings are made explicit

Output requirement:

- warnings must be written clearly
- assumptions must be logged
- validation steps must be recommended

### G3. Conditional Proceed

Meaning:

- progress is allowed only if specific conditions are met

Use when:

- one or two critical issues are unresolved
- the user can move forward only with a bounded test or clearly defined limitation

Example:

- launch this category only as a pilot
- explore distributor conversations only after margin analysis

### G4. Defer

Meaning:

- the idea or step may be valid later, but not now

Use when:

- strategic timing is wrong
- capability is not ready
- focus cost is too high
- the core business is not yet stable

### G5. Block / Validate First

Meaning:

- the system should not recommend moving forward yet

Use when:

- core evidence is missing
- economics are not credible
- customer logic is unclear
- operational failure risk is too high

This is not a rejection of the business forever.

It is a refusal to pretend the step is ready.

---

## 4. Gate Design Principles

### 4.1 Visible Judgment

The system should state clearly why a decision passed or failed a gate.

### 4.2 Evidence-Sensitive

Gate outcomes should depend on evidence quality, not only founder enthusiasm.

### 4.3 Stage-Aware

A weak-evidence early exploration may still justify a small test.

The same weak evidence would not justify a large launch.

### 4.4 Proportionate Rigor

The bigger the decision, the stronger the gate should be.

Examples:

- writing an exploratory memo needs less certainty
- committing to inventory or distributors needs much more certainty

### 4.5 Preserve Momentum Without Faking Confidence

The OS should avoid unnecessary paralysis, but also avoid false certainty.

That is why `Proceed With Warnings` and `Conditional Proceed` both exist.

---

## 5. Decision Types That Require Gates

Not every note needs a gate.

The strongest gates should apply to decisions such as:

- selecting the target customer
- choosing positioning
- finalizing offer structure
- setting pricing
- committing to inventory
- choosing sales channels
- launching a category
- expanding into a new adjacency
- using distributors or wholesale
- importing or taking on high MOQ supply
- scaling paid acquisition

---

## 6. Gate Criteria Categories

Every major decision should be judged across a consistent set of categories.

### 6.1 Strategic Fit

Questions:

- does this align with the business identity
- does this strengthen or dilute focus
- does it fit the current stage

### 6.2 Customer Fit

Questions:

- is there a clear buyer
- is the need credible
- is the buying context understood
- is willingness to pay at least directionally supported

### 6.3 Evidence Strength

Questions:

- what is known vs assumed
- what evidence exists
- is the evidence direct, indirect, or speculative

### 6.4 Economic Logic

Questions:

- can this make money
- do margins support the channel
- is working capital manageable
- are unit economics believable

### 6.5 Operational Feasibility

Questions:

- can the team produce or deliver reliably
- are sourcing, quality, and logistics realistic
- does the business have the capability or partner base needed

### 6.6 Channel Feasibility

Questions:

- is there a realistic path to market
- does the business understand how the buyer will purchase
- can the selected channel be supported operationally and economically

### 6.7 Focus Cost

Questions:

- what will this distract from
- what complexity does it add
- is now the right time relative to other priorities

## 7. Evidence Levels

The OS should classify evidence so gate outcomes are not based on vague confidence.

### E0. No Evidence

- pure idea
- intuition only

### E1. Weak Signal

- anecdotal belief
- a few informal observations
- competitor exists but no direct user signal

### E2. Directional Evidence

- several customer conversations
- category and competitor signal
- prototype or sample feedback
- rough cost model

### E3. Strong Working Evidence

- repeated customer signal
- validated interest
- credible cost structure
- viable operational model
- small pilot success

### E4. Validated Operating Evidence

- real sales
- repeat customers
- actual performance data
- measured margins
- delivery reliability proven

---

## 8. Gate Logic By Stage

### 8.1 Exploration Stage

Default threshold:

- exploratory outputs can proceed at `E0-E1`
- strategic commitments should not

Typical gate outcome:

- proceed with warnings
- conditional proceed

### 8.2 Validation Stage

Default threshold:

- most strategic choices should have at least `E2`

Typical gate outcome:

- proceed with warnings
- conditional proceed
- block if no real customer signal exists

### 8.3 Launch Stage

Default threshold:

- launch-critical decisions should typically have `E2-E3`

Typical gate outcome:

- proceed
- proceed with warnings
- block if pricing or operations are still speculative

### 8.4 Expansion Stage

Default threshold:

- category or channel expansion should usually require `E3` or stronger

Typical gate outcome:

- conditional proceed for pilots
- defer
- block if economics or capability are weak

## 9. Module-Level Gate Expectations

### Founder Context And Intent

Typical gate:

- proceed once founder goals and constraints are clear enough

### Customer Research

Typical gate:

- do not finalize positioning or channels if customer definition is still vague

### Positioning And Value Proposition

Typical gate:

- proceed with warnings if customer and competitor understanding is only directional

### Offer Architecture

Typical gate:

- block full launch planning if the offer itself is still structurally unclear

### Pricing And Unit Economics

Typical gate:

- block major channel commitments if unit economics are not believable

### Operations, Delivery, And Supply System

Typical gate:

- block scale or stocked launch if repeatability and fulfillment readiness are weak

### Route-To-Market And Sales Architecture

Typical gate:

- do not recommend distributors, marketplaces, or offline retail without margin and support logic

### Marketing And Growth System

Typical gate:

- do not recommend serious paid acquisition until message, offer, and conversion readiness are adequate

### Expansion / New Category Smoke Test

Typical gate:

- default to pilot, defer, or block unless strategic fit, economics, and capability are all credible

---

## 10. Example Gate Rules

### Rule 1: Pricing Before Channels

Do not recommend:

- distributors
- wholesale
- low-margin marketplaces

unless pricing and unit economics are already modeled.

### Rule 2: Customer Before Positioning

Do not finalize positioning if the target customer is still too broad or undefined.

### Rule 3: Repeatability Before Scale

Do not recommend scaling a product, service, or channel before repeatability is shown.

### Rule 4: Proof Before Paid Growth

Do not recommend strong paid acquisition before:

- offer clarity
- message clarity
- conversion path clarity

### Rule 5: Pilot Before Expansion

For new categories or channels, prefer pilot recommendations unless strong evidence already exists.

### Rule 6: Focus Before Breadth

If a proposed addition creates major complexity while the current business is unstable, default to defer.

---

## 11. Gate Output Format

Every gated module or workflow should end with a compact decision summary.

Recommended structure:

- decision under review
- gate outcome
- why it received that outcome
- key evidence used
- key assumptions still open
- required next actions

Example:

- Decision: Add wallpaper category
- Outcome: Conditional Proceed
- Reason: strong strategic fit, but sourcing and channel economics remain unproven
- Open assumptions: MOQ burden, installation complexity, buyer demand level
- Next action: run small B2B pilot and compare local vs imported sourcing

---

## 12. Control-System Connection

Gate decisions should update the control artifacts.

### Assumption Log

Should record:

- what assumptions prevented a stronger gate outcome

### Evidence Tracker

Should record:

- what evidence justified the gate decision

### Decision Gate Tracker

Should record:

- outcome
- date
- scope
- condition for revisiting

### Open Questions Register

Should record:

- what must be answered before the decision can change

## 13. Chitr Stress Test

Applied to Chitr, this gate philosophy would help prevent weak strategic moves such as:

- adding too many product families before repeatability is clear
- using distributors before margin logic exists
- launching future wallpaper ideas before sourcing and demand are tested
- spending heavily on growth before product and channel clarity exist

It also supports momentum by allowing:

- early strategic documentation
- pilot recommendations
- phased channel testing

This indicates the gate system is strong for both Chitr's launch and future expansion decisions.
