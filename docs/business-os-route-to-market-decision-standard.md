# Business OS Route-To-Market Decision Standard

Status: Active standard
Purpose: Define the minimum output standard the Business OS must produce whenever it evaluates how a business should reach customers and sell.

## 1. Why This Standard Exists

The Business OS should not stop at listing channel options such as:

- D2C
- assisted D2C
- B2B pipeline
- distributor / wholesale
- offline retail
- exhibitions
- marketplaces
- hybrid

That is too weak.

The system should help the user determine:

- which route-to-market architecture is best for the business now
- which channel should come first
- which channels should come later
- which channels should be deferred
- why the recommended order is stronger than the alternatives

---

## 2. Required Outputs

Any route-to-market decision inside the Business OS should explicitly produce:

1. Recommended Channel Architecture
2. Channel Order Of Operations
3. Channel Decision Gate
4. Why Now / Why Not Now

These outputs are mandatory for:

- M12 Route-To-Market And Sales Architecture
- start-from-scratch workflows once an initial business frame exists
- launch workflows
- pivot workflows
- growth or channel-diagnosis workflows
- expansion workflows where new channels are being considered

---

## 3. Minimum Questions

The system should answer at least the following:

1. Who is the first real buyer?
2. How does that buyer discover options like this?
3. How does that buyer prefer to evaluate and buy?
4. What sales motion does the offer require:
   - self-serve
   - assisted
   - consultative
   - pipeline-led
   - partner-led
5. Which channel architecture best fits the current stage of the business?
6. If hybrid is right, which channel should come first?
7. What must be proven before the next channel is activated?
8. Which channels look attractive but are premature?
9. What are the margin, control, cash-flow, and operational tradeoffs of each path?

---

## 4. Standard Recommendation Shape

Every route-to-market recommendation should be structured like this:

- Recommended channel architecture
- Primary channel first
- Secondary channel later
- Deferred channels
- Why now
- Why not now
- Channel decision gate
- Evidence strength
- Next test or proof required

Example:

- Recommended channel architecture: hybrid
- Primary channel first: assisted D2C
- Secondary channel later: designer-led sales
- Deferred channels: distributors, offline retail
- Why now: highest control, fastest learning, lower channel complexity
- Why not now: distributor margins and reorder expectations are not yet proven
- Channel decision gate: move into distributors only after repeatable packaging, reorder logic, and healthy channel margins are demonstrated
- Evidence strength: E2 directional
- Next test: run a 60-90 day assisted D2C pilot and track inquiries, conversion, margin, and support burden

---

## 5. Decision Policy

The Business OS should not recommend vague hybrid strategies such as:

- do both online and offline
- try D2C and distributors
- start everywhere and see what happens

Instead, if hybrid is recommended, the system must state:

- which channel starts first
- why it starts first
- what second channel is staged behind it
- what evidence or readiness unlocks that second channel

---

## 6. Artifact Policy

The Route-To-Market Strategy Memo should always include:

- Recommended Channel Architecture
- Channel Order Of Operations
- Why Now / Why Not Now
- Channel Decision Gate

If a workflow reaches route-to-market decisions but does not produce those sections, the workflow is incomplete.

---

## 7. Gate Policy

Route-to-market should be treated as a gated decision, not just a descriptive section.

Examples:

- do not recommend distributor-first without margin, payment-cycle, and partner-support readiness
- do not recommend pure D2C if the offer clearly requires assisted trust-building
- do not recommend hybrid without stating first-channel focus
- do not recommend offline or partner expansion if current direct learning is still too weak

---

## 8. Practical Meaning

This standard makes the Business OS stronger because it forces the system to answer:

- what is the best path now
- what comes next later
- what should wait
- what must be true before expansion happens

That is materially more useful than generic channel brainstorming.
